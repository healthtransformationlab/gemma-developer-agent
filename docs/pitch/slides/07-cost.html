What it cost

On four L4 GPUs a fix took 1.8 to 1.9 GPU-hours. Most of a task's time was the model's own calls.

Share of a task's time spent in the model's own calls

66–83%

Another 14–15% went to summarising older turns (context compaction). From the Kaggle runs' timing records (OpenTelemetry spans). 45 of 207 runs ended at the model's context window.

GPU-hours for each fix, on four L4 GPUs

1.9 and 1.8

1.9 with graph retrieval and 1.8 with the test report: about 1.59 and 1.50 US dollars at a median rental of 0.83 an L4-hour. Kaggle's GPUs were free.

Hours of agent time for each fix, on the T4 baseline

0.38 vs 1.80

0.38 on the 13 clearly described tasks it ran, against 1.80 over all 48. Those 13 took 17% of its 9.0 agent-hours and gave 4 of its 5 fixes.

What it means: the time goes to the model's own calls, not to the graph tools, and an hour spent on clearly described tasks bought the most fixes.

Source: the paper, Section 6, "Time, GPUs and cost". The prices are rental estimates, not money spent

The runs kept OpenTelemetry spans: names, counts and seconds, never prompts or replies. In the three Kaggle arms, 66 to 83 percent of a task's time was model calls and 14 to 15 percent was context compaction, which is the harness summarising older turns. The graph tools took under 0.1 percent. Forty-five of 207 runs ended when a prompt passed the model's context window. A fix took 1.9 GPU-hours with graph retrieval and 1.8 with the report, on four L4s: about 1.59 and 1.50 US dollars at a median rental price, though Kaggle's GPUs were free. On the T4 baseline, the 13 core-split tasks it ran took 17 percent of its 9.0 agent-hours and gave 4 of its 5 fixes: 0.38 hours a fix against 1.80 overall.
